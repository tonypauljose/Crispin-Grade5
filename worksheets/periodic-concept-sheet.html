Chapter 1 · Numbers

Putting commas — two systems

  • Indian system (3-2-2): from the right, first comma after 3 digits, then every 2 digits. 12345678 → 1,23,45,678
  • International system (3-3-3): a comma every 3 digits from the right. 12345678 → 12,345,678
  • Indian places: ones, tens, hundreds, thousand, ten-thousand, lakh, ten-lakh, crore, ten-crore.
  • International places: ones, tens, hundreds, thousand, ten-thousand, hundred-thousand, million, ten-million.
  • Quick links: 1 lakh = 100 thousand, 1 crore = 10 million.

Place value vs face value

  • Face value = the digit itself, wherever it sits. (In 53,210 the face value of 3 is just 3.)
  • Place value = digit × its place worth. (In 30,210 the place value of 3 is 3 × 10,000 = 30,000.)
  • The place value of 0 is always 0.

Successor · predecessor · expanded form

  • Successor = +1 (the number just after). Predecessor = −1 (the number just before).
  • Successor of 9,999 = 10,000; predecessor of 10,000 = 9,999.
  • Expanded form breaks a number by place value. 5,34,067 = 5,00,000 + 30,000 + 4,000 + 60 + 7
  • Standard form = add the parts back together.

Comparing, ordering & rounding

  • To compare: more digits = bigger. Same digits? Compare from the left, place by place.
  • Ascending = smallest → largest. Descending = largest → smallest.
  • Rounding rule: look at the digit just to the right of the rounding place. 5 or more → round up; less than 5 → round down (keep it the same).
  • Round 7,839 to nearest 10 → look at 9 → up → 7,840. Round 64,560 to nearest 100 → look at 6 → up → 64,600.

Roman numerals

IVXLCDM
1510501005001000
  • Add when symbols go big → small: VIII = 5+1+1+1 = 8.
  • Subtract when a smaller symbol comes before a bigger one: IV=4, IX=9, XL=40, XC=90, CD=400, CM=900.
  • Never repeat a symbol more than three times in a row.
  • Examples: 46 = XLVI, 72 = LXXII, 90 = XC, 99 = XCIX.

Chapter 2 · The Four Operations

Vocabulary & special properties

  • Addition → answer is the sum. Subtraction → answer is the difference (the number we subtract from is the minuend).
  • Multiplication → answer is the product. Division → answer is the quotient, with a remainder left over.
  • a + 0 = a (0 is the additive identity).   a − 0 = a.   a − a = 0.
  • a × 1 = a (1 is the multiplicative identity).   a × 0 = 0.
  • a ÷ 1 = a (the number itself).   a ÷ a = 1 (for any non-zero a).
  • Order does not matter for + and ×: 432 × 755 = 755 × 432.

Multiplying & dividing by 10, 100, 1000

  • × 10 / 100 / 1000: add one / two / three zeros. (476 × 100 = 47,600.)
  • ÷ 10 / 100 / 1000: the last one / two / three digits become the remainder; the rest is the quotient. (5,678 ÷ 100 → quotient 56, remainder 78.)

Long multiplication & division

  • Multiply by each digit of the second number, shift each row one place left, then add the rows.
  • Divide using long division: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down the next digit — repeat.
  • The remainder is always smaller than the divisor.

⭐ Divide and check (very important!)

  • After any division, verify with: (quotient × divisor) + remainder = dividend.
  • Example: 77,218 ÷ 5 → quotient 15,443, remainder 3. Check: (15,443 × 5) + 3 = 77,215 + 3 = 77,218 ✓ — matches the dividend, so the division is correct.

Word-problem checklist

  • Read twice. Underline the numbers and the question.
  • Decide the operation: altogether / total → + or ×; left / remain / how many more → −; shared equally / each → ÷.
  • Show working, write the answer with its unit (₹, buns, children…), and check it makes sense.
